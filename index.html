of the year

Twenty-one frames, kept where the light found them. Blossom, birch, water and open country, all made on the same long walk through the year.

02 / The archive

What shaped me

Read, rewatched, repeated, rooted for. Seven shelves of things that did not just stay. They built the person who keeps them.

Four of these are still on the shelf because I read them twice in one winter.

Half of these I saw once, in a cinema, at the wrong age to understand them.

Long series here are the ones I stopped midway and finished years later.

There are three hundred and forty-one English tracks in here, and Pop opens the index. Most nights I am somewhere in the Mandarin groups.

A line to return to

Do not go gentle into that good night

Dylan Thomas · A villanelle · 1947

Kept here because the light was never the reward. Catching it, and not letting go, was the whole instruction.

Do not go gentle into that good night,
Old age should burn and rave at close of day;
Rage, rage against the dying of the light.

Though wise men at their end know dark is right,
Because their words had forked no lightning they
Do not go gentle into that good night.

Good men, the last wave by, crying how bright
Their frail deeds might have danced in a green bay,
Rage, rage against the dying of the light.

Wild men who caught and sang the sun in flight,
And learn, too late, they grieved it on its way,
Do not go gentle into that good night.

Grave men, near death, who see with blinding sight
Blind eyes could blaze like meteors and be gay,
Rage, rage against the dying of the light.

And you, my father, there on the sad height,
Curse, bless, me now with your fierce tears, I pray.
Do not go gentle into that good night.
Rage, rage against the dying of the light.
